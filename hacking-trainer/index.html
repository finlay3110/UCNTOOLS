How to play

  1. Pick a cyber defence level. Higher levels mean a bigger grid, more defence routines, and more progress needed before you can apply a partial breach.
  2. Probe a node by tapping it. Your first probe is always safe, and opens up part of the grid.
  3. Read the traces. The number on a revealed node is its trace: how many of the 8 nodes touching it (diagonals too) hide a defence routine. A blank node has none, so its neighbours open by themselves.
  4. Build progress. Hacking in progress is how much of the grid's safe nodes you've cleared. Clear them all for a full breach.
  5. Apply when it's good enough. Once progress passes the level's mark (Basic 60%, Secure 70%, Hardened 80%), Apply banks the breach at that progress. Fortified only accepts a full breach.
  6. Two strikes. The first defence routine you trigger is marked X and you carry on. A second ends the attempt; if you'd passed the Apply mark, the breach still applies what it had. There are no flags, as in Bridge Command: keep track of the routines in your head.

Hack effect vs Scanner heat

  • Every finished attempt costs 20–40° of Scanner heat, however it ended. What you get for that heat depends on how far you got: a full breach adds 50% to the target's hacked level, applying at 70% adds 35%, and a failed attempt adds nothing.
  • The hack wears off steadily: 100% clears in about 3 minutes. Two full breaches reach 100% in theory, but it usually takes three or more, as the first wears off while you work on the next.
  • 100% hacked takes 75 points off the target system: at 100% power it works at 25%, and at 200% it still works at 125%.
  • What a full hack does: beams fire every 24 seconds instead of 6, missiles reload four times slower, and impulse, manoeuvring and warp drop to a quarter. Shields only take about 12.5% more damage, and Scanners are rarely worth it. Pick the system under the meter to see its effect as the hack builds and wears off; switching starts that system from 0%.
  • Scanners don't cool without coolant. Each 10% Engineering gives them cools them 0.5° a second. Report heat past 75°; at 100° they start taking damage. Pick one primary hacker, so two officers don't overheat the same Scanners.

Tips & tricks

  • Start in low-risk areas and use the traces to expand quickly through safe space. Nodes along the edge of the open area touch the fewest hidden nodes, so they give the surest answers.
  • Full house: if a trace has exactly that many hidden nodes around it, every one of them is a defence routine. Leave them all alone.
  • Already satisfied: once you know where all of a trace's routines are, every other hidden node around it is safe. Probe them.
  • 1-2-1 in a straight line: the node in front of the 2 is always safe. The nodes in front of the two 1s may be routines. (In these pictures, ✓ is safe and ? may be a routine.)
  • 1-2-2-1 in a straight line: the nodes in front of the 1s are always safe. If the four nodes behind the pattern are all safe, the nodes in front of the 2s are routines.
  • 1-1 in a straight line, with the nodes behind both safe: the third node in that line is safe, whatever the next number is. All these patterns work flipped or rotated.
  • Compare neighbours: when one trace's hidden nodes are all shared with the trace next to it, any extra nodes the second one touches hold the difference. Equal numbers mean the extras are safe.
  • Count the routines near the end. Each level has a fixed number (shown in the level list). If the ones you know about account for all of them, every other hidden node is safe.
  • When you must guess (it happens on Hardened and Fortified), guess early rather than after lots of work, and pick the node that would tell you the most if it's safe.
  • Speed beats perfection. If Apply is available and the next move is uncertain, bank it. A perfect hack too late is only tidier wreckage.
  • Aim for under 40 seconds on Secure without triggering a single defence routine. The Academy Manual reckons that's very achievable with practice.
  • Challenge a friend after an attempt: the QR code gives their phone the same grid, opening with your first probe, and their result says who won.
